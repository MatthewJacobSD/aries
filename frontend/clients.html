The client workspace

Keep the relationship next to the work.

A client workspace gives both sides a shared place to understand what is happening without exposing the rest of the agency. The client sees what is relevant to their relationship; the agency keeps its internal operations private.

Briefs

Start with the request

Keep current briefs, requirements, and supporting files attached to the client work so everyone is working from the same source.

Approvals

Make decisions visible

Keep questions, feedback, submissions, and approvals connected to the relevant campaign instead of relying on scattered conversations.

Visibility

Share the right information

Give clients access to their campaigns, deliverables, and relevant financial records without exposing unrelated creators, clients, or internal agency data.

Collaboration

Less searching. More shared context.

Email and messaging can still handle conversations. Aries gives the important operational information a durable home, so a new operator does not have to reconstruct the account from old messages and attachments.

Brief

Keep the current version clear

The active brief and its supporting material stay connected to the campaign rather than becoming another attachment lost in a conversation.

Feedback

Keep decisions attached

Questions, requested changes, and approvals remain associated with the work they refer to, making the next action easier to identify.

Handoffs

Make context transferable

When an account moves to another operator, the workspace provides a shared operational history instead of requiring a private briefing from the previous owner.

Records

Keep important activity visible

Campaign changes, approvals, assignments, and relevant events can remain connected to the client record for future reference.

Access control

The client sees their work. The agency sees the whole operation.

Client collaboration only works when the boundary is clear. Aries should separate external visibility from internal workspace access and make permissions explicit.

Create the workspace

Create the client record, connect the relevant campaigns, and assign the internal team responsible for the relationship.

Invite the client

Give approved client users access to their workspace without granting access to the wider agency environment.

Control what is shared

Separate client-visible information from internal notes, operational records, financial details, and other information that should remain private.

Remove access cleanly

When a user's role or the client relationship changes, access can be revoked while the agency retains the underlying operational history.

Connected work

The client relationship connects directly to delivery.

A client should not need a separate system to understand which work is active, waiting for approval, or complete. Campaigns provide the connection between the relationship and the agency's delivery workflow.

Planning

What is being delivered?

Connect campaigns, briefs, timelines, and assigned work directly to the client record.

Approval

What is waiting?

Surface work that needs client feedback or approval so the next action is clear to both sides.

Complete

What has been delivered?

Keep completed campaign activity available as part of the relationship history rather than losing it when the project closes.

Boundaries

Shared does not mean everything is shared.

A useful client workspace has a deliberate boundary between collaboration and internal operations. Visibility should follow the user's role and the purpose of the information.

Client-visible

Relevant briefs, campaign status, deliverables, approvals, shared files, and financial information intended for that client.

Internal-only

Internal notes, other client records, creator information, private operational data, credentials, and agency-only decisions remain outside the client view.
